## When it's used

When a verb has both an indirect and a direct object pronoun: *Me lo dai?* (Will you give it to me?) *Glielo dico domani.* (I'll tell him or her tomorrow.)

## How it's formed

The indirect pronoun comes first and its **i** becomes **e**; *gli* and *le* both become **glie-** and join the next pronoun.

| | lo | la | li | le | ne |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| mi | me lo | me la | me li | me le | me ne |
| ti | te lo | te la | te li | te le | te ne |
| gli, le | glielo | gliela | glieli | gliele | gliene |
| ci | ce lo | ce la | ce li | ce le | ce ne |
| vi | ve lo | ve la | ve li | ve le | ve ne |
| si | se lo | se la | se li | se le | se ne |

They attach to infinitives, gerunds and *tu* imperatives as one word: *dammelo*, *dirglielo*, *spiegandocelo*.
