## When it's used

Every German noun is masculine, feminine or neuter, and the gender is rarely guessable from the meaning: *der Tisch* (table), *die Lampe* (lamp), *das Buch* (book). Learn each noun with its article. Nouns are always written with a capital letter.

## How it's formed

| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Definite | der Tisch | die Lampe | das Buch | die Bücher |
| Indefinite | ein Tisch | eine Lampe | ein Buch | Bücher |
| Negative | kein Tisch | keine Lampe | kein Buch | keine Bücher |

The plural article is always *die*, whatever the gender. The forms above are the nominative; other cases change the article (*Akkusativ*, *Dativ*).

## Helpful patterns

- Compound nouns take the gender of their last part: *das Haus* + *die Tür* = *die Haustür*.
- Nouns ending in *-ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft, -ion* are feminine: *die Zeitung*, *die Freiheit*.
- Nouns ending in *-chen* and *-lein* are neuter: *das Mädchen*, *das Brötchen*.
- No article for professions and nationalities after *sein*: *Ich bin Lehrerin.*
