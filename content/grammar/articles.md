## When it's used

Italian uses the definite article more than English: with general statements (*il caffè è buono*, coffee is good), with possessives (*la mia casa*), and with names of countries (*l'Italia*).

## How it's formed

| | Masculine | Feminine |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Singular, before a consonant | il treno | la casa |
| Singular, before s + consonant, z, gn, ps, y | lo studente | la stazione |
| Singular, before a vowel | l'anno | l'estate |
| Plural, before a consonant | i treni | le case |
| Plural, before s + consonant, z, vowel | gli studenti, gli anni | le estati |

Indefinite: **un** treno, **un** anno, **uno** studente, **una** casa, **un'**estate.

## Watch out

- The article follows the word right after it, not the noun: *lo zio* but *il vecchio zio*.
- *Gli* before any vowel: *gli amici*, *gli zii*.
